# Tutoring

**This folder is for the embedded tutor, not for students.**

Tutoring runs for one hour after every class except Sessions 1 and 12. The instructor does not stay for tutoring — these materials are the tutor's working set.

## What's in each session folder

A typical `tutoring/session-XX/` folder contains:

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `Session##_Tutor_Guide.html` | The session's tutoring activity — what to do, in what order, with timing |
| `Session##_AnswerKey.html` | Full expected answers + screenshots of correct visual state |
| `Session##_Common_Stuck_Points.md` | Where students typically get stuck + how to nudge without giving away |

## Behavioral guidance for the tutor

Every tutor guide includes a **Do / Don't** section at the top. The short version:

**Do:**
- Let students struggle for ~2 minutes before stepping in
- Ask "what have you tried?" before showing
- Have students articulate the goal before clicking
- Use the answer key to confirm a student's correct answer, not to demonstrate
- Note recurring stuck points for the instructor

**Don't:**
- Click on a student's machine; coach them to click
- Skip the take-a-breath break at midpoint
- Move on to the next exercise if half the room is still working
- Read the answer key out loud as instruction
- Treat tutoring time as office hours — it's a structured collaborative session with a specific activity

## Tutoring activity types

Tutoring activities are not just "more lab time." They're a different shape from class labs:

- **Pair builds** — two students share a screen and rotate the keyboard every 10 minutes
- **Detective activities** — students inspect a pre-built `.pbix` and answer questions about what they see
- **Speed challenges** — short timed tasks (15 min) to build fluency
- **Capstone work blocks** — protected time to make progress on the capstone with the tutor circulating

The activity type is named at the top of every tutor guide.
