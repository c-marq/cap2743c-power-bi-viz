The Scenario

Memo — to the BI team

Subject: AdventureWorks year-end executive report

The AdventureWorks leadership team meets at the end of the fiscal year to review company sales performance and decide where next year's investment goes — which product categories, which regions, which sales channels.

They do not want a spreadsheet. They do not want five separate reports. They want one Power BI report they can open in the meeting, read in a few minutes, and ask their own follow-up questions of while everyone is looking at the screen.

Build that report. Make it look like the conclusions matter, and make it answer back when someone in the room asks "what about bikes only?" or "what did Europe do?"

You are the analyst presenting it.

That scenario is the whole assignment. Everything below is the specification for delivering it well.

What You Will Build

One Power BI report (.pbix), built on the AdventureWorks dataset you have used all term, with the following components. Each one maps to labs you will have already completed — see the Capstone Checklist for the exact mapping.

Component Points What it means
Report structure & data story 35 A multi-page report with a clear narrative — an overview page that answers the headline question, and at least one detail page. The pages are ordered the way the story should be read.
Visual selection 35 At least six visuals across the report, each one chosen because it fits the question it answers — the right chart family for the job, not a chart picked at random. Includes at least one map and at least one card.
Formatting & branding 30 A custom theme applied consistently. Conditional formatting used at least once where it earns its place. The report looks convincing, not default.
Interactivity 35 The report answers back: at least two slicers, deliberate editing interactions, and at least one of drill-down, drill-through, or a custom tooltip. The audience can explore without you.
Accessibility 20 The four checks pass: alt text on every visual, a sensible tab order, WCAG AA color contrast, and descriptive visual titles.
Presentation or walkthrough 45 You present the report — live in class, or as a recorded screen walkthrough. You explain the data story, show the interactivity working, and name the hardest part of building it.
Total 200
💡 WHY THESE SIX COMPONENTS Each component is one chapter of the course made visible. Structure and visual selection are Chapters 1–2. Formatting is Chapter 3. Interactivity is Chapter 4. Accessibility is Chapter 3 again. The presentation is you doing what an analyst actually does — standing next to the report and making it matter. If you did the labs, you have done all six already. The capstone just asks for them in one place, polished.

Two Ways to Present — You Choose

The presentation component (45 points) can be delivered two ways. Both are worth the same. There is no penalty for either choice — pick the one that lets you do your best work.

OptionWhat it looks like
Live presentation You present your report to the class in Session 12 (Thursday, June 18). About 4–5 minutes. You walk the data story, click through the interactivity, and answer a question or two.
Recorded walkthrough You record your screen (2–3 minutes) walking through the same thing — the data story, the interactivity, the hardest part. You submit the recording instead of presenting live. Any screen recorder works; the Power BI window plus your voice is all that is needed.
✅ HOW TO DECIDE Presenting live is good practice for a real analyst job, and the room is friendly — it is your own class. But if presenting live would take cognitive energy away from building a strong report, record the walkthrough instead and put that energy into the work. The report is what is being graded. The presentation is how you narrate it. Choose the format that protects the report.

The Reflection

Along with the file, write a short reflection. Three questions, two or three sentences each:

  1. What was the hardest part of building this report?
  2. What would you do differently if you built it again?
  3. What is the one thing you are most proud of in the finished report?

The reflection is part of the presentation/walkthrough component. It is not a trick — there are no wrong answers. It is the analyst's habit of looking back at the work honestly.

Grading

The capstone is graded against the Capstone Rubric — a separate document with Excellent / Good / Needs Work tiers for each of the six components. Read the rubric early; it tells you precisely what "Excellent" looks like for every piece, so there are no surprises.

  • Late policy: 48-hour grace period with no penalty. After 48 hours, 10% per day, capped at a 30% maximum reduction.
  • Redo: if the capstone scores below the Good tier, you may revise and resubmit once, for up to the Good tier score — the same redo policy as the labs.
  • Partial credit is always available. A report that is strong in four components and weak in two still earns the four. Build what you can build well.
