PART 1 — INTERACTIVITY · Chapter 4 · about 1 hour

Step 1 — Set up the report page

✅ DO THIS
  1. Open Power BI Desktop and open the Homework 2 starter .pbix from Canvas.
  2. You should land in Report View on a page named Subcategory Report, with a column chart of Sales Amount by Product Subcategory and a line chart of Sales Amount by Date already on it.
  3. Add a Slicer: from the Visualizations pane click the Slicer icon, then drag Product → Category into its Field well. The slicer fills with the four categories.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Click Bikes in the slicer. Both charts should redraw to Bikes subcategories only. Clear the selection before continuing.

Step 2 — Build a drill-through detail page

✅ DO THIS
  1. Add a new page (click the + by the page tabs) and rename it Subcategory Detail.
  2. On it, build a Bar chart of Sales Amount by Product → Product (the individual product names), and a Card of Sales Amount.
  3. Click empty canvas so no visual is selected. Drag Product → Subcategory into the Drill through well in the Visualizations pane. A Back button appears automatically.
  4. Return to the Subcategory Report page, right-click a subcategory bar, and choose Drill through → Subcategory Detail.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE If the detail page shows every subcategory, the field went into the page filter section instead of the Drill through well. They are different wells. Confirm Subcategory is in Drill through.

Step 3 — Add a bookmark

✅ DO THIS
  1. On the Subcategory Report page, open the Bookmarks pane (View tab → Show panes → Bookmarks).
  2. Set the slicer to Bikes.
  3. Click Add in the Bookmarks pane and rename the new bookmark Bikes View.
  4. Clear the slicer, click Add again, and name that bookmark All Categories.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Click the Bikes View bookmark — the slicer and charts should snap to Bikes. Click All Categories — they return to the full picture. A bookmark saves the slicer state, so this should work cleanly.

PART 2 — ANALYTICS · Chapter 5 · about 1 hour

Step 4 — Add a forecast

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? Part 1 made the report something the audience can question. Part 2 makes it analyze. A forecast answers "what comes next" — and its shaded confidence band is an honest statement of how uncertain the projection is.
✅ DO THIS
  1. On the Subcategory Report page, click the line chart of Sales Amount by Date.
  2. In the Visualizations pane, open the Analytics tab (the magnifying-glass icon).
  3. Open the Forecast section → Add. Set Forecast length to 3 and Confidence interval to 95%.
  4. Leave the confidence band visible.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Do not turn off the confidence band. The band is the honest part of a forecast — removing it makes the report overstate how sure it is.

Step 5 — Write a visual calculation

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A visual calculation is DAX written on a visual, computed on the visual's own rows — one of the two skills new for the January 2026 PL-300 exam. A running total down the rows is a single line of DAX here.
✅ DO THIS
  1. Add a Table visual. Put Product → Subcategory and Sales Amount in it.
  2. With the table selected, go to the Home tab and click New visual calculation.
  3. In the formula bar, type this exactly:
Running Total = RUNNINGSUM([Sales Amount])
  1. Confirm the formula and close the edit screen. The table now has a Running Total column.

PART 3 — NARRATIVE & REFLECTION · about 40 minutes

Step 6 — Add a Smart Narrative and review it

✅ DO THIS
  1. On the Subcategory Report page, click empty canvas so no visual is selected.
  2. In the Visualizations pane, click the Smart narrative icon. Power BI generates a text summary of the page.
  3. Read every sentence carefully. Click into the text — it is editable.
  4. Find at least one sentence you can improve — a number that needs context, or a claim that is too strong — and rewrite it in your own words.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Accepting the generated narrative without reading it. An auto-written summary can state a guess as confidently as a fact. The rule from the chapter: the narrative drafts, the analyst reviews and signs. You are the analyst here — read it, fix it, own it.
