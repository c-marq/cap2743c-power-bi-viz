Part 1 of 3 — Analytics in Action (Chapter 5) 14 points

Part goal: point three Chapter 5 features at the AdventureWorks sales data — one that explains a change, one that projects forward, and one that calculates down a visual's rows.

Task A — Explain a change 4 pts

✅ DO THIS
  1. See it. Build a Clustered column chart. Put Month (Date table) on the X axis and Sales Amount on the Y axis. Each column is a month you can right-click.
  2. Name it. The right-click menu has an Analyze submenu — Explain the increase, Explain the decrease, and Find where this distribution is different.
  3. Find it. Right-click the column for a month that dropped from the month before it → Analyze.
  4. Do it. Choose Explain the decrease. Page through the candidate explanations with the arrows. When one looks like it explains the drop, click the small + to add that chart to your page.
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? The Analyze feature reports statistical contribution — it ranks the factors that moved with the number. It narrows two days of searching to one chart. It does not prove cause; that last check stays yours. You will come back to exactly this distinction in Part 3.

Task B — Forecast forward 4 pts

✅ DO THIS
  1. See it. Build a Line chart. Put Month (Date table) on the X axis and Sales Amount on the Y axis.
  2. Name it. With the line chart selected, the Visualizations pane's third tab is the Analytics pane; its last section is Forecast.
  3. Find it. Visualizations pane → Analytics tab → Forecast → Add.
  4. Do it. Set Forecast length to 3 months and Confidence interval to 95%. The chart extends with a projected line and a shaded band. Leave both showing.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Turning off the shaded confidence band because the chart looks "cleaner" without it. The band is the honest part — like a hurricane forecast cone, it widens because uncertainty grows with distance. A projection line with no band tells the audience the report is more certain than it is. Keep the band.

Task C — A visual calculation 4 pts

✅ DO THIS
  1. See it. Build a Table visual. Put Month (Date table) and Sales Amount into it, so the table lists each month and its sales.
  2. Name it. The control that adds a calculation onto a visual is New visual calculation.
  3. Find it. With the table selected, on the ribbon: Home tab → New visual calculation. An edit screen opens showing the table's rows as a grid.
  4. Do it. In the formula bar, type the line below, confirm it, and close the edit screen. The table now carries a running total that accumulates down the months.
Running Total = RUNNINGSUM([Sales Amount])
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A visual calculation is computed on the visual's own rows, after they are aggregated — which is why a running total is one short line here, instead of the denser CALCULATE pattern a measure would need. RUNNINGSUM can walk the rows because the visual is where the rows live.

Task D — Key Influencers 5 pts

✅ DO THIS
  1. See it. The Visualizations pane's grid of icons includes the Key Influencers icon.
  2. Name it. The Key Influencers visual. With no visual selected, click its icon to add one.
  3. Find it. The visual has an Analyze well and an Explain by well.
  4. Do it. Drag Sales Amount into Analyze. Drag Color (Product table), Business Type (Reseller table), and Country (SalesTerritory table) into Explain by. Read the ranked influencers, then click the Top Segments tab to see combinations of factors that form a high or low group.
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? Key Influencers answers what moves this number? It ranks factors that are associated with Sales Amount going up or down. "Associated with" is the careful phrase — it is correlation, not proven cause. Hold that thought; Written Response 2 is about exactly it.

Part 3 of 3 — Verify Before You Trust 8 points

Part goal: Chapter 6 ends on the rule every AI chapter owes its reader — AI notices, you judge. This Part has no new visual to build. It is the judgment.

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? An AI result is a lead, never a finished finding. Section 6.7 gives you five checks to run on any AI-generated insight before it informs a decision. Running them is the part of the job the exam — and an employer — is really testing.

Written Response 3 — Run the five checks 8 pts

Pick one result from Part 2 — a Key Influencers influencer, or a branch of your decomposition tree. Name which result you chose, then walk it through all five checks from Section 6.7, writing a sentence or two on each:

  1. Business sense — does the result make sense given what you know about a bike company?
  2. Correlation or cause — is this a proven cause, or two things that move together?
  3. Bias — could the data behind it be skewed in a way that produced this result?
  4. Traceable — could you follow the numbers back to the rows they came from?
  5. Sign your name — would you put your initials on this and show it to Marcus?

Finish with one sentence: based on the five checks, would you present this result as-is, present it with a caveat, or hold it back and investigate first?
