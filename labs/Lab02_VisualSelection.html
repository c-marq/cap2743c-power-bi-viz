💡 WHAT THIS LAB IS REALLY ABOUT In the guided demo your instructor chose the visuals for you. This lab flips that. The skill Chapter 2 is built around is choosing — reading a business question and recognizing which visual it is asking for, the way a mechanic reads a job and reaches for the right tool. Five questions, five choices, five builds.

This lab uses a different set of fields than the guided demo on purpose. The demo used Country, Month, and Subcategory. Here you will work with Reseller, Region, and Fiscal Year. Same skills — new specifics.

PHASE 1
Warm-Up — Match the Question to the Family · about 10 minutes

Before you build anything, practice the choosing step. For each question below, pick the visual family from the dropdown. You are not building yet — just deciding.

✅ DO THIS Make a choice for all three. There is a defensible best answer for each — you'll see them confirmed as you build in Phase 2. If two feel close, ask: what is the shape of the answer? A ranked list, a moving line, or a single number?
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Picking the visual that looks most impressive. Chapter 2 is blunt: the question comes first, the shape of the answer second, the visual third. A pie chart is not "better" than a card — it is just built for a different question.
PHASE 2
Build — Five Questions, Five Visuals · about 50 minutes

Now build. Each task gives you a business question. Build the visual on a single report page. Work down the list — by the end you'll have five visuals on one canvas.

Task 1 — "Which resellers brought in the most sales?"

✅ DO THIS
  1. On a blank page, click the Clustered bar chart icon in the Visualizations pane (horizontal bars — reseller names need room).
  2. Expand Reseller in the Data pane. Drag Reseller into the Y-axis field well.
  3. From Sales, drag Total Sales into the X-axis field well.
  4. Sort it: click the … menu on the visual → Sort axis → Total Sales → then Sort descending.
💡 WHY A BAR CHART "Which brought in the most" is a comparison question — a ranked list. Bar charts encode value as position and length, the two channels the eye reads most accurately. The sort is what makes the ranking instant.

Task 2 — "How did total sales move across the fiscal years?"

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas to deselect Task 1.
  2. Click the Line chart icon (the zigzag).
  3. Expand Date in the Data pane. Drag Fiscal Year into the X-axis field well.
  4. From Sales, drag Total Sales into the Y-axis field well.
💡 WHY A LINE CHART "Move across the years" is a time question. The connecting line encodes the rate of change as slope — the eye reads a climb or a drop instantly. A line shows the whole trajectory as one shape.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE If your line has only one or two points, check the X-axis — you want Fiscal Year, which gives one point per year. If you accidentally used the full Date field you'll get a dense daily line instead.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK — Screenshot Required Your line chart should show one point per fiscal year, connected into a line. Take a screenshot and paste it below.

Task 3 — "What is our total sales figure for the whole company?"

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas.
  2. Click the Card icon — the rectangle with a small lightning bolt. (Power BI updated the card visual in late 2025; the lightning bolt just marks the current version.)
  3. From Sales, drag Total Sales into the Data field well.
  4. The card displays one large number — the company-wide total.
💡 WHY A CARD "What is our total" is a one-number question. It doesn't need a chart — it needs to be big, clear, and unmissable. The card is the headline-number tool.

Task 4 — "How do sales compare across the sales regions?"

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas.
  2. This is a comparison question — build a Clustered column chart (vertical; region names are short).
  3. From Sales Territory, drag Region into the X-axis field well.
  4. From Sales, drag Total Sales into the Y-axis field well.
  5. Sort it descending using the … menu, the way you did in Task 1.
💡 WHY A COLUMN CHART HERE Same family as Task 1 — comparison — but Task 1 had many resellers with long names, so a horizontal bar fit better. Regions are few and short-named, so a vertical column works cleanly. The choice between bar and column is about how many categories and how long the labels are.

Task 5 — The Judgment Call

✅ DO THIS Marcus asks: "Show me how our two sales channels — Reseller and Internet — split the total sales." That is two categories. Build the visual you think fits best, then defend your choice in the reflection below. Build it from the Channel field in the Sales Order table and Total Sales.
💡 THINK IT THROUGH Two categories, and the question is about how they split a whole. Chapter 2 says a pie chart is defensible when there are two or three slices of clearly different sizes. Is this one of the rare cases a pie works? Or would a bar still be the safer call? There is a reasonable answer either way — what matters is that you can defend it.
