PHASE 1
Build the Card Row · about 30 minutes

Start with the three headline numbers. They go across the top of the page — the place a reader's eye lands first.

Task 1 — Card: Total Sales

✅ DO THIS
  1. On a blank report page, click the Card icon in the Visualizations pane — the rectangle with a small lightning bolt.
  2. From the Sales table, drag Total Sales into the Data field well.
  3. The card shows one large number. Drag it to the top-LEFT of the page.
💡 WHY THE LIGHTNING BOLT Power BI updated the Card visual in late 2025. The icon now carries a small lightning bolt to mark it as the current version. It does more than the old card — images, extra formatting — but for a headline number it works exactly the same: one field in, one number out.

Task 2 — Card: Order Quantity

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas to deselect Task 1's card.
  2. Click the Card icon again — a second blank card appears.
  3. From the Sales table, drag Order Quantity into the Data field well.
  4. Place this card to the RIGHT of the first one, the same size.

Task 3 — Card: Order Count

✅ DO THIS
  1. Deselect, then add a third Card.
  2. From the Sales Order table, drag Sales Order into the Data field well.
  3. Click the small dropdown arrow next to Sales Order in the field well and choose Count — you want how many orders, not a list.
  4. Place this card to the right of the second one. You now have three cards in a row.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE If a card shows a tiny number like "1," or a long string of text, you dragged the wrong kind of field, or Power BI is not aggregating it. For Task 3, make sure the field well says Count of Sales Order — not just "Sales Order." A card needs an aggregated number.

PHASE 2
Build the Filled Map · about 30 minutes

Task 4 — Filled Map: Sales by Country

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas, BELOW the card row.
  2. In the Visualizations pane, click the Filled map icon — a globe with shaded regions.
  3. From the Sales Territory table, drag Country into the Location field well.
  4. From the Sales table, drag Total Sales into the Tooltips field well. Power BI shades each country by that value.
  5. Resize the map so it fills the space below the cards.
💡 WHY A FILLED MAP A filled map (a choropleth) shades whole regions — darker for higher. Use it when your geography is areas: countries, states, counties. The map answers "where," not "rank." If the question were "which three countries sold the most," a sorted bar would be the sharper tool — but the question here is geographic pattern.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE If the map draws but no countries are shaded, Power BI may not recognize the location field. Click the Country field in the Data pane, go to the Column tools ribbon, and check that its Data category is set to Country/Region. Giving Power BI the geographic hint fixes most map problems.
