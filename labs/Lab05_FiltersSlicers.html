PHASE 2 — BUILD · about 20 minutes

Step 2 — Add a report filter

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A filter has a scope — how much of the report it reaches. The three layers are Filters on this visual, Filters on this page, and Filters on all pages. The widest one — Filters on all pages — is the report filter. It cleans every page at once. You will use it to keep one year's data in view across both pages.
✅ DO THIS
  1. Make sure you are on the Overview page (check the tab at the bottom).
  2. See it. The Filter pane is the vertical panel on the right of Report View. It has three grey section headers.
  3. Find it. If the pane is closed: View tab → Show panes group → Filters.
  4. Do it. Locate the bottom section, Filters on all pages. From the Data pane, drag Year into that section.
  5. In the filter, tick a single year — 2020.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Use the plain calendar Year field — not Fiscal Year. Also: be sure you dropped Year into Filters on all pages, not Filters on this page. A filter at the wrong scope is silent — Power BI will not warn you. If only one page changed, you used the page-level section by mistake. Drag the field out and try again in the right section.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Click between the Overview and Detail tabs. Both pages should now show 2020 data only — and you never touched a single visual. That is the report filter working.

Step 3 — Add two slicers to the Overview page

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? The Filter pane is the analyst's tool — your audience will not open it. A slicer is a filter promoted to a visual on the canvas, in plain sight, that the audience can click. This is how a manager pulls "Bikes only" out of the report without calling you.
✅ DO THIS — slicer 1: Product Category
  1. Make sure you are on the Overview page.
  2. Click an empty part of the canvas so no visual is selected.
  3. See it. In the Visualizations pane, find the icon shaped like a small funnel — the Slicer.
  4. Do it. Click the Slicer icon. A blank slicer appears on the canvas.
  5. From the Data pane, drag Product → Category into the slicer's Field well.
  6. The slicer fills with four items: Accessories, Bikes, Clothing, Components.
✅ DO THIS — slicer 2: Country
  1. Still on the Overview page, click an empty part of the canvas again.
  2. Click the Slicer icon to add a second slicer.
  3. From the Data pane, drag Sales Territory → Country into its Field well.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Click Bikes in the Category slicer. Every other visual on the Overview page should redraw to Bikes only, and Bikes should show a filled, selected state. If nothing changed, confirm the slicer has a field in its well — an empty slicer draws on the canvas but filters nothing. Clear the selection again before the next step.

PHASE 3 — CONFIGURE · about 25 minutes

Step 4 — Sync the slicers across both pages

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A slicer filters its own page only, unless you tell it otherwise. If a manager sets the Category slicer on Overview and clicks to Detail, the Detail page still shows every category — because Detail does not have that slicer. Sync slicers links one slicer's selection across pages, so a choice on one page follows the audience everywhere.
✅ DO THIS
  1. On the Overview page, click the Category slicer once to select it.
  2. Find it. Click the View tab → Show panes group → Sync slicers.
  3. The Sync slicers pane opens. It shows a row for each page — Overview and Detail — with two checkboxes: sync and visible.
  4. For the Detail row, tick the sync checkbox. (Leave visible unticked — the Detail page does not need the slicer drawn on it, only obeyed.)
  5. Now click the Country slicer and repeat: tick sync for the Detail row.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK On the Overview page, click Bikes in the Category slicer. Now click the Detail page tab. The Detail table should show Bikes data only — even though there is no slicer drawn on the Detail page. That is sync slicers working. Clear the selection when done.

Step 5 — Set one editing interaction

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? Every visual on a page is clickable, and by default a click changes the other visuals. Editing interactions lets you control that — Filter (the other visual refilters), Highlight (it dims the non-matching part), or None (it ignores the click). A card showing the company total is on the page to be a fixed reference — it should not move when someone clicks a bar.
✅ DO THIS
  1. Go to the Overview page.
  2. Click the column chart (Sales Amount by Category) to select it.
  3. Find it. A contextual Format tab appears on the ribbon while a visual is selected. On that tab, in the Interactions group, click Edit interactions.
  4. Small icons now appear on the corner of every other visual on the page.
  5. On the card (total Sales Amount), click the None icon (a circle with a line through it).
  6. Click Edit interactions again to leave editing mode.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE A card only shows two interaction icons — Filter and None — not three. A card has nothing to dim, so Highlight does not apply. If you expected three icons and see two, that is the visual type, not a bug. Pick None.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Click a bar in the column chart — say, Bikes. The chart filters, but the card stays at the full company total and does not change. That is the None interaction doing its job: the total stays a fixed reference no matter what the audience clicks.

Step 6 — Add a drill-down hierarchy

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A hierarchy is a field with built-in levels. Sales Territory has a Group level, then Country inside it, then Region inside that. Put a hierarchy on a chart's axis and the audience can drill down — start at the top level, then expand one branch a level deeper, all without leaving the visual or the page.
✅ DO THIS
  1. On the Detail page, click an empty part of the canvas, then add a new Column chart from the Visualizations pane.
  2. From the Data pane, expand the Sales Territory table. Drag Group, then Country, then Region — in that order — onto the chart's X-axis well. The three fields stack to form a hierarchy.
  3. Drag Sales Amount onto the Y-axis well.
  4. See it. Small arrow icons appear in the top corner of the chart.
  5. Do it. Click the drill-mode arrow (the downward split-arrow) to turn drill mode on. Then click one column — for example, Europe. The chart redraws to show the countries inside Europe.
  6. Click the drill-up arrow to climb back to the top level.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Drill-down only works when the axis has more than one field stacked on it. If clicking a column does nothing, check the X-axis well — you need Group, Country, and Region all in it. One field alone gives you no levels to drill into.
