Lab 9 — Mobile Layout & the Distribution-Ready Report

Session 10 · Thursday, June 11, 2026 · Block B  |  15 points  |  Chapter 7 — Beyond the Report

Building a report is half the job. The other half is getting it to land — the right shape, on the right screen, in front of the right person. In the Block A demo you watched a report get published, pinned to a dashboard, and given an alert. This lab is the part of distribution that lives in Power BI Desktop, on your own machine: a clean monitor page, a phone layout, and the setting that lets readers explore on their own.

💡 WHY THIS LAB IS DESKTOP-ONLY Publishing, dashboards, and data alerts all live in the Power BI Service — the cloud — and the Service needs an organization account this course does not provide. That work is real and it is on the PL-300 exam, so you saw it demonstrated in Block A and you will answer questions about it here. What you build today is everything distribution needs that does not require the Service. It is also the part most under an analyst's own control.

Phase 1 of 4 — Setup 1 point

Phase goal: open the starter file and add the page you will build on.

✅ DO THIS
  1. See it. The starter file opens in Report View — the canvas, the Visualizations pane on the right, and the Data pane listing the AdventureWorks tables.
  2. Name it. The strip of tabs along the bottom is where report pages live.
  3. Find it. Find the + at the end of the page tabs.
  4. Do it. Click + to add a new page. Right-click its tab → Rename Page → type Monitor.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK You have a blank page named Monitor open, and the Data pane on the right lists tables including Sales, Product, Date, and SalesTerritory. If the Data pane is empty, the model did not load — ask the instructor before going on.
Phase 2 of 4 — Build the Monitor Page 5 points

Phase goal: build a single screen that does the job a dashboard would — the few numbers a busy manager checks at a glance. You will place four visuals.

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? A dashboard is a single screen of the things that matter most. You cannot build a Service dashboard without an account — so you build the same thinking here as a report page: one screen, four visuals, no scrolling, no clutter. This page is what a manager would glance at every morning.
✅ DO THIS — place four visuals on the Monitor page
  1. Card. Add a Card visual (the new card — its icon carries a small lightning bolt). Put Order Quantity in it. This is the headline number.
  2. Column chart. Add a Clustered column chart. Put Category (Product table) on the X axis and Sales Amount on the Y axis.
  3. Line chart. Add a Line chart. Put Year (Date table) on the X axis and Order Quantity on the Y axis. This is the trend over time.
  4. Bar chart. Add a Clustered bar chart. Put Channel (Sales table) on the Y axis and Sales Amount on the X axis.
  5. Tidy it. Arrange the four visuals so all of them fit on the screen at once with no overlap and no scrolling. Put the card in a top corner where the eye lands first.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Crowding the page with more visuals "just in case." A monitor page earns its value from restraint. Four well-placed visuals that fit on one screen beat eight that force the reader to scroll. If it does not fit without scrolling, make the visuals smaller — do not add a second page.

Phase 3 of 4 — Mobile Layout 4 points

Phase goal: build a phone-shaped version of the Monitor page, so a manager walking through an airport sees a usable screen instead of a report they must pinch and pan.

✅ DO THIS
  1. See it. The ribbon's View tab has a group for layout options.
  2. Name it. The control is Mobile layout.
  3. Find it. Make sure the Monitor page is open, then go to View tab → Mobile layout. The canvas becomes a tall phone frame with a grid, and your visuals wait in a side panel.
  4. Do it. Drag the Order Quantity card and the Order Quantity by Year line chart onto the phone grid — card at the top, trend below it. Size them large enough to read on a phone. Then return to View tab → Desktop layout.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE Dragging all four visuals onto the phone grid. A phone layout is not the desktop page shrunk — it is a deliberate choice of the few things that matter in the thirty seconds a walking user gives it. Two large, readable visuals beat four tiny ones. The desktop version still has all four; it is untouched.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK In Mobile layout view, the phone frame shows your card and your trend line, both large enough to read. Switching to Desktop layout shows the full four-visual page, unchanged. You now have two layouts in one file.

Phase 4 of 4 — Ready to Hand Off 5 points

Phase goal: turn on the one self-service setting that does not need the Service, then think through the distribution choices you would make with a full account.

Task — Enable Personalize Visuals 1 pt

✅ DO THIS
  1. See it. The File menu, top-left, opens Options and settings.
  2. Name it. The setting is Personalize visuals, under this file's report settings.
  3. Find it. File → Options and settings → Options → in the left list, under Current file, click Report settings.
  4. Do it. Tick the box for Personalize visuals, then click OK.
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? Personalize Visuals lets a reader reshape a visual — swap a field, change the chart type — into a private view that never changes the report for anyone else. It is the one piece of distribution control that lives in Desktop, so it is the one you can hand off without the Service.

Written Response 1 — Choose the container 2 pts

AdventureWorks' field sales team is about 40 people who spend their day in cars and stores, checking numbers on their phones. Marcus wants a glance at the headline figures; the analysts on his team want to explore the detail. Power BI gives you three containers — a report, a dashboard, and an app. Which container fits which audience, and why? Name and explain at least two of the three.

Written Response 2 — Keep the audience current 2 pts

Suppose you did have a Power BI Service account. Your Monitor page needs to reach the field team without anyone remembering to reopen it. You have three tools: a data alert, an email subscription, and automatic page refresh. Pick the one that best fits this field team. Name what triggers it (a condition, a clock, or a timer) and explain why your choice fits a team that lives on phones.
